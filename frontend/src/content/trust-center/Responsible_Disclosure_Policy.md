## 1. Our Commitment

CyberSec values the work of independent security researchers in helping us keep CyberSec Toolkit (the "Platform") secure. This Policy describes how to report a suspected security vulnerability to us, what you can expect from us in return, and the rules we ask researchers to follow.

## 2. Scope

The following are in scope for vulnerability reports:

- The CyberSec Toolkit web application, including its dashboard and account features
- Publicly accessible CyberSec Toolkit API endpoints
- Authentication and session handling on the Platform

## 3. Out of Scope

The following are generally out of scope for this Policy:

- Denial-of-service (DoS or DDoS) testing against the Platform or its infrastructure
- Social engineering of CyberSec personnel, contractors, or users
- Physical security testing of any facility
- Automated, high-volume scanning that degrades Platform performance for other users
- Vulnerabilities in third-party services we rely on that are not under our control (report these directly to the relevant provider)
- Issues that require unlikely user interaction combined with a compromised or physically accessed device
- Missing security headers or best-practice recommendations without a demonstrated, exploitable impact
- Reports generated solely by automated scanning tools without manual verification of impact

If you are unsure whether something is in scope, report it and we will let you know.

## 4. How to Report a Vulnerability

Send a report to our security contact by email:

**Security Email:** cybersecbuisness@gmail.com

Please use a clear subject line indicating this is a security report (for example, "Security Report: [brief description]").

## 5. What to Include in Your Report

To help us investigate efficiently, please include:

- A description of the vulnerability and its potential impact
- Steps to reproduce the issue, including any request/response data, screenshots, or a proof-of-concept
- The URL, endpoint, or feature affected
- Any tools used to identify the issue
- Your contact information, if you would like to be credited or kept informed of our progress

## 6. Our Response Process

When we receive a report, we aim to:

1. Acknowledge receipt of your report within a reasonable timeframe
2. Investigate and validate the reported issue
3. Keep you informed of our progress at reasonable intervals
4. Notify you once the issue has been remediated or if we have determined it is not a valid vulnerability, with an explanation
5. Discuss an appropriate disclosure timeline with you before any public disclosure

Response times may vary depending on the complexity of the report and our current workload, as we are a small team.

## 7. Rules of Engagement

When testing the Platform, please:

- Only test against your own account and data, or data you are otherwise authorized to access
- Avoid accessing, modifying, or deleting data belonging to other users
- Stop testing and report immediately if you gain unintended access to data that is not yours
- Avoid actions that could degrade the availability of the Platform for other users
- Do not publicly disclose a vulnerability before we have had a reasonable opportunity to address it, as described in Section 10

## 8. Safe Harbor

We will not pursue legal action against researchers who make a good-faith effort to comply with this Policy, including its scope and rules of engagement, while identifying and reporting a vulnerability to us. If a third party initiates legal action related to activity conducted in accordance with this Policy, we will make it known that your actions were conducted in compliance with this Policy.

This safe harbor does not apply to testing conducted outside the scope defined in Section 2, or in violation of the rules described in Section 7.

## 9. Recognition

We do not currently operate a paid bug bounty program. Where a researcher wishes to be credited, and we agree the report is valid, we are happy to acknowledge their contribution, subject to their consent and our discretion regarding the form of acknowledgment.

## 10. Coordinated Disclosure Timeline

We ask that researchers give us a reasonable period, generally up to 90 days from the date of a valid report, to investigate and remediate an issue before any public disclosure. We are open to discussing this timeline on a case-by-case basis, particularly for complex issues, and will communicate proactively if more time is needed.
